# Audit and restructure documentation

Use this workflow for mixed pages, documentation sets, migrations, and information-architecture reviews.

## Inventory reader needs

For each page or coherent passage, record:

- intended reader and competence
- situation: learning or working
- need: action or knowledge
- promised outcome
- current evidence and authority
- likely primary Diátaxis mode

Classify content at passage level before classifying directories. Existing names are evidence, not truth.

## Find problems

Look for:

- tutorial steps interrupted by theory, exhaustive options, or production branches
- how-to guides that teach basics or document tools without a real user goal
- reference entries interrupted by task narratives or speculative rationale
- explanation pages overtaken by procedures or catalogs
- unlabeled assumptions, stale facts, untested commands, broken links, or inconsistent terms
- important reader needs with no clear destination

Treat a blurry boundary as a diagnosis prompt, not an automatic demand to split. A short prerequisite, warning, or rationale can support the primary need without changing the page's mode.

## Improve incrementally

1. Choose the smallest coherent unit that can be improved and verified.
2. Restate its reader need and primary mode.
3. Remove, relocate, or link substantial material serving other needs.
4. Close gaps exposed by the edit.
5. Verify facts and examples.
6. Publish or commit the coherent improvement.
7. Repeat.

Do not create an empty four-part hierarchy as a substitute for useful content. Let repeated, well-classified improvements reveal the structure the documentation needs.

## Design navigation

Use landing pages and READMEs to orient and route. Organize navigation around recognizable reader needs and domain concepts, not only internal component names.

Do not require readers to understand Diátaxis terminology. Labels such as “Learn”, “Tasks”, “Reference”, and “Concepts” can work, but domain-specific titles are often clearer. Make the destination and outcome of each link explicit.

Allow readers to enter anywhere. Diátaxis describes recurring needs, not a mandatory reading order.

## Report an audit

Lead with the most consequential user-facing findings. For each finding, state:

- location
- observed content and likely reader need
- mismatch or quality risk
- recommended smallest coherent change
- evidence or verification needed

Separate verified factual defects from structural judgments. Rank by impact on task success, safety, discoverability, and maintenance cost.

## Review the resulting set

Confirm that:

- each important need has a clear home
- neighboring pages hand readers off intentionally
- navigation supports both learning and work
- reference structure makes gaps visible
- tutorials remain runnable end to end
- how-to guides solve named real-world goals
- explanatory pages develop coherent mental models
- no mode is treated as a completeness quota
